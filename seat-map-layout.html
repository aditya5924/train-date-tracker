Understanding Indian Railways Seating Layouts

Finding out your exact berth or seat location before boarding the train makes traveling much more convenient. Whether you are traveling overnight in a Sleeper coach or taking a daytime Shatabdi Chair Car, the IRCTC ticketing system relies on specific mathematical seating patterns.

Overnight Trains: Sleeper (SL) and 3-Tier AC (3A)

These coaches are divided into cabins, containing a total of 72 to 80 berths. The seating pattern repeats in blocks of 8 seats:

  • Lower Berth (LB): Seat numbers ending in 1 and 4.
  • Middle Berth (MB): Seat numbers ending in 2 and 5.
  • Upper Berth (UB): Seat numbers ending in 3 and 6.
  • Side Lower (SL): The highly sought-after seats by the window along the aisle (ending in 7).
  • Side Upper (SU): The upper seat along the aisle (ending in 8).

Overnight Trains: 2-Tier AC (2A)

2-Tier AC coaches offer more privacy, lacking the middle berth entirely. The pattern repeats in blocks of 6 seats (Lower, Upper, Side Lower, Side Upper).

Daytime Trains: AC Chair Car (CC) & Executive (EC)

Found on Shatabdi, Vande Bharat, and Intercity expresses, these coaches feature airplane-style seating:

  • Executive Chair Car (EC): Premium 2x2 layout. You will either be assigned a Window seat or an Aisle seat. The pattern repeats every 4 seats.
  • AC Chair Car (CC): Standard 3x2 layout. Features a 3-seat block (Window, Middle, Aisle) on one side, and a 2-seat block (Aisle, Window) on the other.
  • Second Sitting (2S): The most economical daytime seating with a dense 3x3 layout. You will be seated in a Window, Middle, or Aisle position in a block of 6.
