IRCTC Ticket Cancellation & Refund Policy Guide

Plans change, and sometimes cancelling a train journey is inevitable. However, the Indian Railways (IRCTC) refund rules are highly dependent on exactly when you hit the cancellation button and the current status of your ticket. This guide breaks down the financial deductions so you know exactly how much of your money will be refunded.

1. Cancellation Charges for Confirmed Tickets

If your ticket is fully confirmed, the amount deducted depends entirely on how many hours before the scheduled departure of the train you cancel the ticket. The IRCTC uses a tiered deduction system:

  • 48 Hours or more before departure: A flat cancellation fee is deducted per passenger based on the travel class (details in the table below).
  • Between 48 hours and 12 hours before departure: 25% of the base fare is deducted, subject to the minimum flat rate mentioned below.
  • Between 12 hours and 4 hours before departure (or up to chart preparation): 50% of the base fare is deducted, subject to the minimum flat rate.
  • Less than 4 hours before departure: No refund is granted for confirmed tickets.

2. Flat Cancellation Rates (Per Passenger)

If you cancel well in advance (more than 48 hours before the train's scheduled departure), these are the flat fees deducted by IRCTC:

Ticket Class Cancellation Charge (Per Passenger)
AC First Class (1A) / Executive Class ₹240 + GST
AC 2-Tier (2A) / First Class ₹200 + GST
AC 3-Tier (3A) / AC Chair Car (CC) / 3E ₹180 + GST
Sleeper Class (SL) ₹120
Second Sitting (2S) ₹60

3. RAC and Waitlisted (WL) Tickets

The rules are much more forgiving for passengers who do not have a confirmed berth:

  • Cancellation before chart preparation: Only a nominal clerkage charge of ₹60 per passenger (plus GST for AC classes) is deducted. The rest of the fare is refunded to your original payment method.
  • Auto-cancellation: If you booked your ticket online (e-ticket) and your status remains Waitlisted after the final chart is prepared, the ticket is automatically cancelled by IRCTC. You do not need to do anything. The full amount (minus the ₹60 clerkage fee) is automatically refunded within 3-5 working days.

4. Tatkal Cancellation Policy

The Tatkal quota is designed for emergency travel, and as such, it comes with strict financial penalties for cancellation:

  • Confirmed Tatkal Tickets: No refund is granted. The cancellation charge is 100%.
  • Waitlisted / RAC Tatkal Tickets: If cancelled before chart preparation, the standard clerkage charge (₹60) is deducted, and the remaining fare is refunded.
  • Late Trains: If your train is delayed by more than 3 hours at the journey originating station, you can claim a full refund even on a confirmed Tatkal ticket by filing a TDR (Ticket Deposit Receipt).

5. What is a TDR (Ticket Deposit Receipt)?

If you miss your train, or if there is a severe deficiency in service (such as the AC failing in an AC coach, or the train being diverted), you cannot cancel the ticket normally. Instead, you must file a TDR online through your IRCTC account. TDRs must usually be filed within 1 hour of the train's departure for confirmed tickets, or up to 3 hours for delayed trains. The refund processing for TDRs takes longer (up to 60 days) because it requires manual verification by the Chief Commercial Manager (CCM) of the specific railway zone.
