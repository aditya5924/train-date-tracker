Cancellation & Refund Guide

  • Confirmed Tatkal Tickets: No refund is granted (Zero refund) upon cancellation.
  • Waitlisted / RAC Tatkal: If cancelled before chart preparation, basic clerkage charges (₹60 + GST per passenger) are deducted and the remaining amount is refunded.

If you cancel the ticket at least 48 hours before the scheduled departure, the flat deduction per passenger is:
  • AC 1st Class / Executive: ₹240 + GST
  • AC 2 Tier / 1st Class: ₹200 + GST
  • AC 3 Tier / Chair Car: ₹180 + GST
  • Sleeper Class: ₹120
  • Second Class: ₹60
Note: If cancelled less than 48 hours before departure, cancellation charges can increase to 25% or 50% of the ticket fare depending on the exact timing.

Planning a train journey across Indian Railways requires meticulous timing, especially given the high volume of daily commuters and peak festive rushes. According to the Indian Railways Advance Reservation Period (ARP) guidelines, general reservation tickets can be booked up to 60 days in advance (excluding the date of journey).

The Ultimate Guide to the 60-Day Advance Reservation Period (ARP)

Millions of passengers travel via the Indian Railways network daily, making ticket availability highly competitive. To ensure a fair booking process, the Ministry of Railways established the Advance Reservation Period (ARP). Currently, the general ARP allows passengers to book train tickets exactly 60 days ahead of the journey date. This rule is crucial for planning long-distance travel, especially during peak seasons like Diwali, Holi, and summer vacations.

How is the 60-Day Booking Window Calculated?

Calculating the exact date your booking window opens can be mathematically confusing. The 60-day count excludes the actual date of the journey. Because different months have 28, 30, or 31 days (and leap years introduce further variation in February), simply subtracting two months from your travel date will often give you the wrong opening date. Missing the exact opening day by even 24 hours usually means your desired class (like 3AC or Sleeper) will slip into the Waitlist (WL) or Reservation Against Cancellation (RAC) status.

Why Did Indian Railways Set the ARP to 60 Days?

Historically, the Advance Reservation Period has fluctuated. It was previously set at 120 days, but the authorities reduced it to 60 days to discourage touts and prevent the mass hoarding of tickets. A shorter window ensures that genuine passengers who are certain about their travel plans have a better chance of securing confirmed berths. It also significantly reduces the cancellation rate, streamlining the administrative load on the IRCTC servers.

Exceptions to the 60-Day Booking Rule

While the standard 60-day rule applies to the vast majority of Express and Mail trains, there are a few notable exceptions. Certain daytime intercity trains (like the Taj Express or Gomti Express) have shorter ARPs, often restricted to 15 or 30 days. Additionally, foreign tourists have a special quota with a 365-day advance booking window. However, for the average domestic traveler booking general quota tickets, the 60-day tracker remains the ultimate standard for securing confirmed seats.
